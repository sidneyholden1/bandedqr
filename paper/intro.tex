\section{Introduction}

Spectral and tau methods discretise differential operators into matrices that are
\emph{almost banded}: a banded bulk together with a small, fixed number of dense
rows (the boundary conditions) and dense columns (the tau
corrections)~\cite{olver2013}. Such systems are routinely solved by sparse LU
factorisation---as, for example, in the widely used Dedalus
framework~\cite{burns2020}. Partial pivoting, however, makes the fill
\emph{data dependent}: the cost is unpredictable and the factors may densify well
beyond the original sparsity. A banded QR factorisation instead has
\emph{deterministic} fill---bounded by the bandwidth, with no pivoting---and is
well conditioned. Olver and Townsend~\cite{olver2013} showed that the almost-banded
QR is both fast and well conditioned; yet, to our knowledge, it is not available as
a drop-in, type-generic solver, and the boundary-row sparsification that
underlies its fastest variant has been carried out only analytically, basis by
basis.

We present \texttt{bandqr}, a compact banded and almost-banded QR solver, and make
three contributions. First, we detect the linear recurrence that a boundary row
satisfies \emph{automatically and numerically}---as the null space of a Hankel
matrix formed from the row's values---and use it to collapse the boundary rows into
the band. This requires no analytic knowledge of the basis: the recurrence is read
off the numbers. Second, the factorisation is \emph{deterministic} and pivot-free:
its fill and cost are bounded by the bandwidth and independent of the matrix values,
where sparse-LU pivoting is data dependent and may densify. Carrying the complete
almost-banded structure---dense boundary rows \emph{and} tau columns---it reproduces
the direct solution to machine precision and is competitive with the production
sparse-LU solver of Dedalus, matching its factorisation time and overtaking it
modestly at large~$N$, while remaining unconditionally backward stable. Third, we
provide a unified
implementation: several factorisations behind one interface (Givens, Householder,
recurrence-collapse, and carried-boundary QR), real and complex arithmetic generated
from a single templated kernel, and a batched GPU backend for the
many-independent-systems regime of multidimensional spectral solves.

We validate \texttt{bandqr} against the sparse-LU solver of Dedalus, the banded
Householder QR of \texttt{pybanded}~\cite{pybanded}, and the reference almost-banded
QR of \texttt{ApproxFun}~\cite{olver_approxfun}, and characterise its performance
across problem size, bandwidth, conditioning, and batch size. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the factorisations and the automatic
recurrence detection; Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} reports the benchmarks;
Section~\ref{sec:dedalus} discusses integration into a production spectral solver and
applications.
